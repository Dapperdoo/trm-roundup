THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football today. So you'll do.

Right. No fixtures. Nothing. A complete footballing void. The tournament has reached that strange, breathless pause before the Final, and since there are no players to talk about, we're going to talk about the people who picked them. Ladies and gentlemen, your thirteen managers. Pull up a chair. This is going to take a minute.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting first with 461 points and a lead that is, frankly, starting to look a bit rude. Shatner's Bassoon, first place, a man who spends his spare time in furious, doomed combat with AI tools that refuse to do what they promise. The irony being, of course, that the AI never works for Joe A either — and yet here he is, top of the league anyway. Tristan is breathing down his neck in third, 97 points back, glaring at Jez's spreadsheet and manually cross-referencing every number with the FIFA website like a man who simply does not trust anyone. Which, given this league, is probably correct.

Nick is sandwiched in second — 370 points, very loud, very tall — a man whose shooting action on a football pitch resembles someone trying to knock a seagull off a lamppost with a nine iron. He'll tell you it's power. It is not power. It is chaos. And his tackling is somehow worse: late, reckless, borderline archaeological in how deep he goes in. His main rival is Tom, who is currently eighth, 49 points behind, which means Tom is losing the personal battle as well as the general one. Tom has been trying to win this league for years. He has not won this league. He has, however, been to a great many fields.

Sam is fourth, 361 points, which is an excellent return for a man who once signed a year-long lease on a house because the viewing had its own bar — only to discover on moving day that the property contained no bathroom whatsoever. Sam looked at those empty bar barrels and made his peace with the situation. He is, in the end, an adaptable man. His brother Wigs is tenth, 319 points, gentle and gregarious and doing just fine, thank you — the two of them separated by six places and the kind of sibling dynamic where neither will admit they're keeping score but both absolutely are.

Jeremy is fifth on 360 points, which is exactly where you'd expect a man of his calibre to be — quietly, efficiently, infuriatingly competent. He built the spreadsheet this entire league runs on. He could, if he wanted, build an algorithm to pick his team. He has chosen not to. He picks on instinct, taste and experience, like a man selecting wine, and he does not explain himself to anyone. He watches Lloydy's algorithmic adventures with the expression of a professor watching a student try to eat soup with a fork.

Which brings us to Chris. Ninth place, 320 points, the mad scientist of the group — a man who bikes, travels, builds his own electrical kit, and recently unleashed an AI transfer algorithm that responded to its first live deployment by firing five consecutive bids for players from the 2022 World Cup. Four years out of date. Gone. Portugal, for instance, are already eliminated — their players now exactly the kind of vintage names Lloydy's bot keeps trying to recruit. Jake, who has been loudly suspicious of Lloydy's AI activities for months, received this news with the energy of a man who has been vindicated by God personally. 'SNACOB'S LADDER!' he reportedly shouted, to no one in particular, before injuring himself celebrating.

Jake is twelfth on 292 points, which is a difficult position made more complicated by the fact that a significant chunk of his budget went on Harry Kane, and another significant chunk of his emotional energy is currently devoted to a grudge against Jeremy over a transfer correction that every other manager accepted and moved on from approximately two days ago. Jake has not moved on. Jake is still there. Jake also went to the dentist recently, which he loved — genuinely, unironically loved — and probably came back and told his team about it at length. He is, in fairness, a man of enthusiasms. Wind turbines. Mushrooms. Dental hygiene. Shouting his own team name. Dancing with a full tray of drinks until the drinks are on someone else.

Dan is seventh, 329 points, living off-grid, extremely witty, historically excellent at this game — his main ally Malik is anchoring the table in thirteenth on 280 points, signing anyone who has ever worn a Manchester United or Portugal shirt, which is a bold strategy in a tournament from which Portugal have now been eliminated. Malik is managing from Iceland, which at least gives him a philosophical distance from the carnage. Dave is eleventh on 300 points, a poet and lecturer who loves wordplay, city breaks, and a dream of hosting a Culture Show segment called 'Trossy's Wafflecock' — a title he has clearly thought about more carefully than his current league position. And Joe S, sixth on 338 points, Back of the Van United, ex-pro, universally beloved, a man whose playing career spent more time in the physio room than on the pitch. He is baffled by most technology. He is doing better than several people who are not baffled by technology. There is a lesson there, probably.

The Final is coming. One match left. Everything still to play for — or at least, everything still to be anxiously refreshed on a spreadsheet, cross-referenced with a FIFA webpage, and argued about in a group chat at eleven o'clock at night. See you on the other side.

The Final is the last fixture standing, and when it arrives, this league ends with it. Thirteen managers, one trophy, and absolutely no more time for sentiment. Get your squads in order. Jake, please put the tray down.
