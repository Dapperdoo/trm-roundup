THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football today. You're the entertainment now.

Right. There are no fixtures today. The World Cup has paused for breath, the pitches are empty, the VAR officials are finally getting some sleep, and we — your faithful bulletin — have absolutely nothing to report on. Which means we're turning the spotlight away from the football and directly onto the thirteen people who've been making catastrophically confident decisions about it for the past several weeks. Pull up a chair. This is the bit where we take the piss, warmly and at length, out of every single one of you.

Let's start with the man at the top, because he's been up there long enough that it's starting to feel suspicious. Joe A — Shatner's Bassoon, 461 points, a full 91 clear of second place — would like you to know that he is absolutely on top of this. He is not on top of this. He is currently three browser tabs deep into an argument with an AI chatbot that promised to build him a player-scouting model and has instead produced a haiku about Lamine Yamal and a pie chart with no labels. Joe A's entire week off from football has been spent trying to get a machine to do something useful, which, as a man who uses these things professionally, he should know by now is a fool's errand. Tristan, meanwhile — third place, 364 points, doing it all by hand from the FIFA website like a man transcribing the Bible with a biro — watches this unfold with the quiet satisfaction of someone who has never once trusted a shortcut. He throws his hands up. He shakes his head. He reaches for another sweet. The rivalry between these two is essentially the rivalry between a man who believes in systems and a man who believes in himself, and right now both of them are losing to the lull.

Directly between them in the table sits Nick — second, 370 points — who has spent the international break doing what Nick does: eating something enormous, saying something very loud in a very deep voice, and almost certainly launching some kind of physical challenge that ends with someone else on the floor. On a football pitch, Nick is what you'd call a 'presence'. Technically you'd call him a 'danger to public safety', but 'presence' is the polite version. His shooting is the stuff of legend — specifically the kind of legend that involves a ball clearing the stadium roof and landing in a car park three streets away. His rival Tom — eighth, 321 points — is, by all available intelligence, somewhere between here and the horizon, probably in a field, probably surrounded by people who are extremely happy about something and cannot fully explain why. One source reached for comment produced the phrase 'the frequencies, man, they're like — yeah.' We'll take that as a positive sign.

Fourth place belongs to Sam, which is genuinely impressive for a man whose approach to major life decisions can be summarised by the fact that he once signed a year-long lease on a house because the viewing had its own bar, only to discover on moving day that the property contained no bathroom whatsoever. Sam looked at that bar. He looked at those empty barrels. He made his peace with it. This is, when you think about it, exactly the kind of lateral thinking that gets you to fourth in a thirteen-man fantasy league. His brother Wigs — tenth, 319 points, separated by a single point from Chris in ninth — is the gentler, more measured O'Shea, the one who listens carefully before speaking and probably had a quiet word with Sam about the bathroom situation that went completely unheeded. Brothers.

Fifth place: Jeremy. The Professor. The man who built the spreadsheet that the entire league still runs on — a spreadsheet Tristan feeds by hand, line by painstaking line, from the FIFA site, which Jeremy finds either flattering or faintly absurd depending on the day. Jeremy picks his team on instinct, on taste, on decades of accumulated football knowledge, and he does it without an algorithm, without a model, and without any of the digital scaffolding that other people in this league have been waving around like a magic wand. He could build one. He simply chooses not to. He watches Chris — Lloydy, ninth, 320 points — and his much-discussed AI transfer engine with the expression of a man watching someone try to open a tin of beans with a particle accelerator. The algorithm, you'll recall, spent the transfer window filing bids for players from the 2022 World Cup before eventually, almost accidentally, landing one valid signing. Chris maintains this was a feature, not a bug. Jeremy has not said anything. He doesn't need to.

And then there's Jake. Snacob's Ladder — he'll remind you of that, loudly, at any moment — sitting twelfth on 292 points, which is not where he planned to be, though in fairness his plans have been somewhat disrupted by the ongoing physical comedy of his own body. Jake injures himself at a rate that would concern medical professionals. He has hurt himself doing nothing. He has hurt himself doing less than nothing. He once injured himself on the way to the dentist, which is either the most Jake thing that has ever happened or a sign that the universe has a very specific grudge. The dentist, for the record, he loves. Genuinely, enthusiastically, without irony. He will tell you about his last appointment. He will tell you about the next one. 'Snacob's Ladder!' he will shout, for no reason, mid-sentence, and then continue talking about oral hygiene. His real enemy, though, is not his own skeleton — it's Chris, and specifically Chris's algorithm, which Jake believes is the tip of a very sinister iceberg. What else is Lloydy cooking up in that office complex of his? What is the lab producing? Jake has theories. Jake has many theories. Jake cannot let the transfer thing go either — Jeremy made an honest mistake, every other manager shrugged, Jake is still filing the paperwork.

Sixth place goes to Joe S — Back of the Van United, 338 points — a man of genuine, undeniable footballing talent who spent a significant portion of his actual playing career horizontal on a treatment table while his body staged a series of elaborate protests. The flair was real. The injuries were also very real, and very frequent, and very creative. Joe S has made peace with this. He has not made peace with modern technology, which continues to baffle him at a fundamental level, and he approaches it with the same cheerful bewilderment he probably brought to the physio's more experimental recovery methods. Seventh place: Dan — Denton Burn, 329 points — living off-grid, thinking clearly, allied with Malik at the bottom on 280, who is managing Propaganda Parade from Iceland and has built his squad with the simple, elegant philosophy of signing anyone who has ever pulled on a Manchester United or Portugal shirt. Portugal, of course, are out of the tournament. Malik is aware of this. Malik is fine. Dan is probably the only person in this league who saw that coming. Finally, Dave — Trossy's Giants, eleventh, 300 points — lecturer, poet, lover of a good pun, occasional cymbal player, and a man who still genuinely believes that one day The Culture Show will call and offer him his own segment. He has the name ready. He has the material. He has, frankly, more than enough waffle for a full series. The crossbar is not the only thing in this league that's been hit and missed.

So there we have it. Thirteen managers, zero fixtures, one very long afternoon. Portugal are gone, France are gone, Argentina are gone, Brazil are gone — half the world has been eliminated and we are barrelling toward a final that will either vindicate someone's carefully constructed squad or make a complete mockery of it. The football returns soon enough, and when it does, these thirteen will be back at their screens, their spreadsheets, their AI tools, their FIFA tabs, their instincts, their injury updates, and — in at least one case — their dental appointment reminders. We'll be ready.

The next fixtures are coming. The standings will shift. Someone will surge, someone will crumble, and Jake will shout 'Snacob's Ladder!' into the void regardless of how either of those things goes. The Morning After will be here.
