THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football. Just thirteen people to mock.

Good morning. There are no fixtures today. The World Cup has paused, the pitches are empty, the VAR officials are finally getting some sleep, and we — your faithful daily bulletin — have absolutely nothing to report. Which means we're turning the spotlight away from the players and directly onto the thirteen individuals who have been shouting at them from their sofas for the past several weeks. Pull up a chair. This is the bit where the managers become the entertainment.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting on 461 points and a 91-point lead, and he would like you to know that this is the result of careful planning and shrewd recruitment. It is not. It is the result of a few decent early signings, a favourable fixture run, and an enormous amount of time that Joe has spent arguing with AI tools that promised him the world and delivered a spinning loading icon. He has spent more hours trying to get a chatbot to do something useful than most people spend on an actual hobby. The chatbot has not yet delivered. Joe's points tally has. We live in a confusing universe.

Breathing down his neck — just about — are Nick and Tristan, which is a sentence that sounds like the start of a buddy-cop film neither of them agreed to star in. Nick is second, which is remarkable for a man whose primary footballing contribution is launching shots approximately forty feet over the crossbar as if he's attempting to dislodge a satellite. On the pitch he's a reckless, flailing menace — the kind of tackler who goes in with both feet and a complete absence of remorse — and yet here he sits, second in the table, large as life and twice as loud. Tristan, third, is furious about this in the specific way that only Tristan can be furious: hands in the air, eyes wide, the expression of a man who has hand-checked every single number on the FIFA website himself and still cannot quite believe the scoreline.

Sam is fourth, which is exactly the sort of position a man achieves when he brings the same energy to fantasy football that he brings to a Shakespeare matinée followed by a long evening in a pub with a good beer garden. Witty, scatty, and absolutely committed to the experience of it all — Sam once signed a year's lease on a house because the viewing had its own bar, only to discover on moving day that the property contained no bathroom whatsoever. He looked at the empty barrels and reportedly did not rule anything out. This is the same instinct that has him fourth in a thirteen-man league. Somehow it keeps working. His brother Wigs is tenth, which Sam has definitely mentioned at least once this week, probably in a group chat, probably with a gif.

Jeremy is fifth, and Jeremy would like it noted — quietly, without fuss, in the manner of a man who finds ostentation slightly vulgar — that he has achieved this without an algorithm, without a model, without so much as a spreadsheet of his own. He built the league's original results spreadsheet from scratch, yes, but his team? Pure instinct. Taste. Experience. The organic approach. He watches Lloydy's AI misadventures with the expression of a professor observing a student try to microwave a salad. Lloydy, for his part, is ninth — one point ahead of Wigs — and his algorithm's most recent contribution to this league was to fire off five consecutive bids for players from the 2022 World Cup before eventually stumbling onto a valid one. Portugal, by the way, are eliminated. Which means several of those 2022-era ghosts the algorithm was chasing have now been joined by their 2026 successors in the tournament graveyard. The bot was simply ahead of its time. Or behind it. Genuinely hard to tell.

Joe S is sixth, which is a fine position for a man who spent a significant portion of his actual professional football career horizontal on a treatment table. Joe was a genuinely gifted player — ask anyone — but the physio room knew his name better than most of his teammates did. He has carried this tradition into civilian life with admirable consistency. Dan is seventh, managing from somewhere off-grid, probably on a hillside, almost certainly with an opinion about it. Dan and Malik are allies, which is a pairing that makes complete sense once you've met them both: one smart and alternative, one quirky and Icelandic, both absolutely certain they know something the rest of the room doesn't. Malik is last, on 280, having assembled a squad with the guiding principle that if a player has ever worn a Manchester United or Portugal shirt, he's worth a punt. Portugal are out. This has been noted.

Dave is eleventh, still composing the perfect pun about it, still dreaming of the day a television commissioner calls and says yes, we'd like to give you a segment, we're thinking something called Trossy's Wafflecock, can you do Thursdays? Dave can do Thursdays. Dave has been ready for Thursdays for years. Tom is eighth, and we have it on reasonable authority that he is fine — happy, in fact — though the source was not entirely coherent and the message arrived in three parts at three separate hours of the morning. He plays psy-trance. He is somewhere. He is having a time.

And then there is Jake. Jake is twelfth, thirty-seven points off the top four, sitting on a squad built around Harry Kane and a collection of budget options assembled with the optimism of a man who has never once let financial reality dampen his enthusiasm. Jake has injured himself doing essentially nothing — the standout being the occasion he hurt himself on the way to the dentist, which he was extremely excited to attend. He loves the dentist. He will tell you about it. He is currently furious at Jeremy for a transfer correction made a couple of days ago that every other manager accepted and moved on from, and he is furious at Lloydy for reasons that have expanded well beyond the algorithm and now encompass Lloydy's entire office complex and what Jake suspects is happening inside it. 'SNACOB'S LADDER!' he shouts, to no one in particular, steadying a tray of drinks that will not survive the evening. He is, genuinely, one of the best people in this league. The tray does not stand a chance.

That's your lot. No fixtures to preview because — as the data will tell you — there is no confirmed next fixture date in the system yet. But the Final is coming, the last teams are standing, and when the football returns, so does this bulletin. Get some rest. Jake, please put the tray down.
