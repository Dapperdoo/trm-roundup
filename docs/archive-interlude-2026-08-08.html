THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football today. The managers are the entertainment.

Right, there are no fixtures today. The World Cup has paused for breath — we are, extraordinarily, at the Final stage, which means every eliminated nation's players are now precisely the sort of bygone names Lloydy's algorithm has been trying to sign all tournament — and since there is nothing on the pitch to report, we are turning the spotlight where it arguably belongs: on the thirteen deeply questionable individuals who have been running these fantasy teams. Consider this a roast. A warm one. Mostly.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting on 461 points and a 91-point lead over second place, and he would like you to know that he is absolutely not smug about this. He is merely quietly confident. Serenely unbothered. The picture of composure. He has spent the entire tournament simultaneously leading the league AND trying to get an AI chatbot to do something — anything — that it actually promised it could do, and failing on the second count every single time. The man is essentially running two parallel projects: one is going brilliantly, one is a slow-motion disaster. He has not yet worked out which is which. His main rival Tristan is breathing down his neck in third, 97 points back, throwing his hands up in disgust at the gap every time he checks the FIFA website by hand, which he does, meticulously, one number at a time, like a man doing a tax return in 1987.

Speaking of rivals: Nick is in second. One hundred and ninety-one centimetres of loud, reckless, beer-fuelled chaos, currently sitting pretty at 370 points. On the football pitch Nick tackles like a man who has just been told the other team personally wronged him — late, high, deliberate, and somehow always surprised when the referee produces a card. His shooting, meanwhile, is the stuff of legend. Not good legend. The kind of legend where the ball is last seen clearing the stadium roof and landing in a car park three streets away. His main rival is Tom, who is in eighth on 321 points and — look, we're not going to say he's at a festival, we've said that enough — but wherever Tom is right now, the music is extremely repetitive, the crowd is extremely happy, and any eyewitness report coming back from that location is, at best, enthusiastically incoherent.

Fourth place belongs to Sam, and Sam deserves a moment. Stage performer, Shakespeare devotee, cricket man, and owner of what is genuinely the best team name in the league. Sam is the kind of person who makes everything look effortless and then, on one specific legendary evening, fell over so comprehensively that both shoes departed his feet simultaneously, as if they had pre-arranged an exit strategy. His brother Wigs is down in tenth on 319 points — counsellor, cricketer, gentle giant — and the fact that the two brothers are separated by 42 points and five places is something neither of them will mention at Christmas but both of them are absolutely thinking about.

Fifth place: Jeremy. The professor. The man who built the spreadsheet that runs this entire league from scratch, who could write an algorithm in his sleep, and who has looked at Lloydy's AI transfer catastrophe — five bids for players from the 2022 World Cup, the whole window held hostage, a bot rummaging through history like it was holding a séance — and said absolutely nothing, because nothing needs to be said. The expression on Jeremy's face when he heard about it has been described by those present as 'a man watching someone use a calculator to boil a kettle.' He picks his team on instinct, taste and experience. It is working.

Chris — Lloydy — is ninth on 320 points and has had a complicated tournament. The algorithm is the algorithm; we all know what happened; Jake certainly won't let anyone forget. But here is the thing about Lloydy: he is also biking, travelling, building electrical things, and generally refusing to sit still, so even when the machine is misfiring he is personally having a tremendous time. Meanwhile Jake — Snacob's Ladder, as he will remind you unprompted, loudly, in any room — is twelfth on 292 points, still seething about Jeremy's transfer correction from a couple of days ago that every other manager shrugged off and moved on from, still convinced Lloydy is running some kind of AI operation across his entire office complex, and still — STILL — carrying the financial weight of Harry Kane like a man who bought a grand piano for a bedsit. Jake injured himself recently. We don't know how. We rarely do. There is a non-zero chance it happened on the way to the dentist, which he loves. He genuinely loves the dentist. He talks about it.

Joe S — sixth, 338 points — is the most naturally gifted footballer in this room, a fact that makes his injury record all the more cosmically unfair. The man spent half his playing career on a treatment table. His fantasy team is doing fine, which is more than can be said for whatever joint or muscle is currently staging a protest. Dan is seventh on 329 points: off-grid, sharp, quietly excellent at this, allied with Malik who is anchoring thirteenth on 280 points and has spent the tournament signing anyone who has ever pulled on a Manchester United or Portugal shirt — a strategy that has become significantly harder now that Portugal are eliminated, because Malik's entire scouting network has just been made redundant.

That leaves Dave in eleventh on 300 points — lecturer, poet, cymbal enthusiast, and a man who has never stopped dreaming of the day he gets his own segment on The Culture Show. 'Trossy's Wafflecock,' it would be called. He has the title. He has the content. He has the heartfelt conviction that highbrow cultural rambling means something. The BBC has not yet called. Dave remains ready.

The Final is on the horizon — one match, one winner, and thirteen managers all suddenly very invested in nations they may or may not have players from. The standings will move. The grudges will deepen. Jake will shout his team name. Someone will get injured doing nothing. The Morning After will be back with actual football. Probably.
