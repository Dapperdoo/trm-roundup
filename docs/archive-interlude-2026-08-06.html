THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football today. The managers are the entertainment.

Right. There are no fixtures today. The World Cup has reached that strange, breathless pause before the Final, and with nothing on the pitch to report, The Morning After turns its lens — slowly, deliberately, with great affection and absolutely no mercy — onto the thirteen people who have been suffering through this tournament alongside you. Pull up a chair. This is the room.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting there at 461 points, ninety-one clear of second place, looking for all the world like a man who has this completely under control. He doesn't. What he has is a large chunk of his waking life devoted to arguing with AI tools that promise him the earth and deliver a spinning loading icon. He has tabs open right now — dozens of them — on some chatbot that swore it could optimise his squad and has instead been confidently wrong about everything for three weeks. The lead is real. The method is chaos. Tristan, 97 points back in third and absolutely seething, would like you to know that he has been doing this by hand — every single number pulled manually from the FIFA website, no shortcuts, no algorithm, just graft and fury — and that this is deeply, cosmically unfair.

Speaking of fury: Nick is in second. Nick, who on a football pitch is less a player and more a weather event — six feet of reckless momentum flying into challenges that were never on, leaving opponents in a crumpled heap while he strides away looking mildly surprised. His shooting is the stuff of local legend: a wild, full-body golf swing that sends the ball approximately into the car park behind the goal. Somehow, second place. His main rival Tom is down in eighth, which would bother Tom more if Tom could be entirely sure what day it is. Reports from the field are, as ever, incoherent but cheerful.

Now. The brothers. Sam is fourth, which is a fine place to be, and he carries it with the easy confidence of a man who once signed a twelve-month lease on a house because the viewing had its own bar — only to discover on moving-in day that the property contained no bathroom whatsoever. He adapted. He always adapts. His brother Wigs is tenth, counselling himself through it with characteristic gentleness, though one suspects the cricket is providing more comfort than the football right now. They are, in their own way, a perfect comedy duo: one who falls over so hard his shoes leave separately, one who absorbs all of life's chaos with a warm smile and a raised eyebrow. The league is lucky to have both of them.

Jeremy is fifth, and he would like to remind everyone — quietly, without making a fuss — that he built the spreadsheet this entire operation runs on. Not with an algorithm. Not with AI. With his brain, his experience, and a set of opinions formed over years of watching football. He picks his team the same way: instinct, taste, knowledge. He could automate it. He has chosen not to. He watches Chris's algorithmic adventures from a distance with the expression of a man observing someone try to assemble flat-pack furniture using only a spoon. Chris, ninth, is the mad scientist to Jeremy's professor — never still, always building something, currently rebuilding his reputation after his beloved transfer algorithm spent an entire window trying to sign players from the 2022 World Cup. Five bids. Five ghosts. One accidental valid pick who, to be fair, scored 8. The lab remains open.

Which brings us to Jake, twelfth, who has been vindicated about the algorithm and cannot stop telling everyone. 'SNACOB'S LADDER!' he announces, to no one in particular, before launching into another theory about what Lloydy is secretly building in his office complex. Jake has also, in the past week, managed to injure himself. We do not know how. We rarely do. What we know is that it happened nowhere near a football pitch, and that the only appointment on his calendar he is genuinely excited about is the dentist. Meanwhile, Harry Kane — the budget-busting marquee signing who ate most of Jake's transfer fund and left him filling the rest of the squad with determined optimists — is out of the tournament, Portugal eliminated, Kane's World Cup over. Jake is processing this on the dancefloor, tray of drinks in hand, and someone nearby is going to get wet.

Joe S is sixth, and the man is a talent. A genuine, bona fide, ex-professional footballer with more flair in one ankle than most of us have in our entire bodies — which is fortunate, because that ankle has, at various points in his career, been the only part of him not in a medical boot. Joe S spent roughly half his playing days horizontal on a treatment table, which is an extraordinary achievement for someone so gifted. He has pivoted to fantasy football with the same instincts and the same relationship with technology, which is to say: none. The phone is a mystery. The app is a deeper mystery. The points, however, are real.

Dave is eleventh, which is not where he wanted to be, but Dave has the soul of a poet and the patience of a man who knows that culture cannot be rushed. He is somewhere right now, probably on a city break, probably eating something interesting, probably composing a pun about the tournament that is three layers deep and lands on a wordplay so baroque it requires a diagram. One day, Trossy's Wafflecock will have its own slot on The Culture Show. One day. Dan, seventh, is doing considerably better — living off-grid, doing it his own way, allied with Malik in thirteenth, who is managing from Iceland and has spent the tournament signing anyone who has ever pulled on a Manchester United or Portugal shirt. Portugal are out. This is not a great time for Malik's transfer philosophy, but he and Dan will regroup, and they will have opinions about it.

So there we are. Thirteen managers, one table, no football today, and a Final still to come. Joe A leads. Tristan is furious. Jake is injured, vindicated, and loudly announcing his team name. The algorithm is in a corner, trying to sign Xavi. And somewhere out there, a dancefloor is about to get a drink spilled on it.

The Final arrives soon, and with it, the last points any of us will score in this tournament. Whatever happens on that pitch will settle the table. Until then: rest up, stay hydrated, and if you see Jake with a tray, move.
