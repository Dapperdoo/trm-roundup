THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football today. You lot will do nicely.

Right. No fixtures. Not a single one. The World Cup has ground to a halt for a day, the pitches are empty, the VAR room is dark, and thirteen grown adults are sat somewhere refreshing a points table that isn't moving. So in the absence of actual football to report on, we're going to do something the algorithm never could: turn the camera round and point it at the managers. Settle in. This is going to sting a little — but only because it's true.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting on 461 points and a lead that is, frankly, obscene. Shatner's Bassoon, first place, ninety-one points clear of second. The man is an actor, a Guinness drinker, and by his own admission a cheerful disaster zone in terms of personal organisation — and yet here he is, top of the league, while the rest of you have been agonising over spreadsheets. His real hobby, of course, is spending hours trying to get AI tools to actually do what they promise. He opens a chatbot, types a perfectly reasonable request, and three hours later he's still there, negotiating, cajoling, explaining what he meant the first time. The AI has promised him sixteen different things and delivered approximately none of them. He should be furious. Instead he's first in the league and mildly amused. Tristan is coming for him, though. Tristan is always coming.

Speaking of Tristan — third place, 364 points, and he gleans every single number by hand from the FIFA website like a man transcribing the Dead Sea Scrolls. No shortcuts. No siphoning. Just Tristan, a browser tab, and the grim satisfaction of doing it properly. He throws his hands up in disgust at least four times per matchday, which is either a character flaw or a coping mechanism, and at this point nobody can tell which. Nick is sandwiched between them in second, which is remarkable for a man whose entire footballing identity is a wild golf swing of a shot that clears the crossbar, the stadium, and several nearby postcodes. On the pitch Nick is a reckless, dangerous, badly-timed tackle waiting to happen — frequently on purpose — and his opponents know it. Somehow this translates into 370 fantasy points. The chaos works. Don't ask how.

Sam is fourth. Sam, whose team name is an instruction — Look at his face. Just Look at his FACE! — and whose face is, genuinely, worth looking at. He is a Shakespearean, a cricket man, a beer man, a dancer, and a person who once signed a year-long lease on a house because the viewing had its own bar, only to discover on moving-in day that there was no bathroom. No bathroom. A full bar, no bathroom. He looked at the empty barrels and made his calculations. Sam is fine. Sam is always fine. Wigs, his brother, is tenth — which means the family WhatsApp has a very clear power dynamic right now, and we can only imagine what Sam's face looks like when he checks the table.

Fifth is Jeremy — the Professor, the man who built the spreadsheet that underpins this entire operation. Brilliant programmer, devastating 5-a-side tackler, picks his team entirely on instinct and taste and has never once handed that decision to a machine. He doesn't need to. He just knows. He watches Lloydy's algorithm fire five bids for players from the 2022 World Cup with the quiet, withering expression of a man who has always known exactly what happens when you let a computer think for you. He says nothing. He doesn't have to.

Which brings us, inevitably, to Chris. Lloydy. Ninth place. The mad scientist. The man whose algorithm — his pride, his baby, his lab project — held the entire league's transfer window hostage while it attempted to sign players who had, in some cases, already retired. Five bids. Five. All from 2022. The bot wasn't scouting the tournament; it was holding a séance. It eventually landed one valid bid, and to be fair, that player scored 8 points. One out of six. Lloydy called it a success. Nobody else did. Meanwhile, Jake — twelfth, 292 points, still seething — has been vindicated so completely that he has started saying 'I told you so' in his sleep. Jake, who injured himself on the way to the dentist. On the way. To the dentist. Not at the dentist — before he even got there. He loves the dentist, by the way. Absolutely adores it. Will tell you about his last check-up at length, unprompted, at a party, while holding a tray of drinks that is already listing dangerously to one side. He also cannot let go of the transfer dispute from a couple of days ago — Jeremy quietly corrected an honest mistake, every other manager nodded and moved on, and Jake has filed it away as a personal grievance he will be nursing until at least the next World Cup. 'SNACOB'S LADDER!' he shouts, to no one in particular, because he needs the reminder.

Joe S is sixth. Back of the Van United. Universally beloved, genuinely gifted, and the owner of a career that spent more time in the physio room than on the pitch. The man was an ex-pro whose body treated football as a hostile environment. He is also, famously, baffled by modern technology in a way that is both endearing and spectacular — the kind of man who treats a smartphone like it's a Rubik's cube that occasionally rings. Dan is seventh, off-grid and unbothered, which is exactly the right energy for a musician who has historically been very good at this and is quietly biding his time. His ally Malik is thirteenth — last, 280 points — managing from Iceland, signing anyone who has ever pulled on a Manchester United or Portugal shirt, which is a transfer strategy and also, at this point, a cry for help. Portugal, of course, are eliminated. Malik is reviewing his options. His options are limited.

Dave is eleventh. Trossy's Giants. Poet, lecturer, lover of puns and city breaks and cigarettes, and the man who still dreams of presenting a segment on The Culture Show called 'Trossy's Wafflecock', in which he would hold forth on cultural nothingness with great sincerity and a pint in hand. He plays the cymbals, but only on special occasions. Nobody knows which occasions qualify. Tom is eighth — Anamaduwa Athletic, 321 points — and has never won this league despite years of trying, which is the sort of fact that sounds like a joke until you realise it's just a fact. He plays psy-trance and only psy-trance, and somewhere right now there is a witness to his weekend who is trying to describe what they saw but the words aren't quite coming out right. They seem happy, though. Very happy.

That's your thirteen. Bruised, beloved, and collectively responsible for one of the more chaotic fantasy leagues in living memory. The World Cup Final is on the horizon — one match left, one last chance for points, glory, bragging rights, and the settling of several outstanding scores. The pitches will be live again soon. Until then: stay hydrated, Jake — and for the love of God, put the drinks tray down.
