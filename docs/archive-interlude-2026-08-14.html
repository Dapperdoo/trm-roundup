THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No ball, no mercy — just thirteen managers in the dock

Right. There are no fixtures today. The World Cup has paused for breath, the players are icing their hamstrings, and the nation — well, thirteen people in a WhatsApp group — has nothing to argue about. So we're turning the spotlight away from the pitch and directly onto the people who've been shouting at it. Pull up a chair. This is the bit where the football stops and the roasting begins.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting on 461 points and an expression of mild, vaguely surprised satisfaction — like a man who accidentally sat on the TV remote and changed the channel to exactly what he wanted. Joe A, first in the league, spends the rest of his waking hours locked in furious combat with AI tools that promise him the world and deliver a spinning cursor. He is, in a very real sense, the only person in this league who is simultaneously winning a fantasy football tournament AND losing a war against a chatbot. Tristan, right behind him in third, would like everyone to know he has done all of this by hand — every single data point pulled manually from the FIFA website, one by one, like a man laying a patio with a teaspoon. He throws his hands up in disgust at least four times a day. At Joe A, mostly. The feeling is mutual.

Nick is in second. Nick is always somewhere near the top, which is baffling to anyone who has watched him play actual football — a man whose shooting technique most closely resembles a six-foot-four golfer attempting a bunker shot in a gale. Every effort sails serenely over the bar, over the stand, over the car park, and into the general direction of low Earth orbit. On the pitch he is also a reckless, clattering menace in the tackle — not accidentally, just enthusiastically — leaving opponents in a heap while looking genuinely puzzled about what all the fuss is. Tom, his main rival, is currently eight places below him in eighth. Tom would be furious about this, but Tom is — well, Tom is somewhere. There was a report. Incoherent. Very happy. Something about the sky being the wrong colour. He seemed fine.

Down in sixth, Joe S is nursing something. He's always nursing something. The man had one of the most naturally gifted careers in the game and spent approximately half of it horizontal in a physio room, staring at the ceiling, while lesser players ran about freely on pitches he should have owned. The treatment table knows his name. It has his preferred pillow. And yet somehow he remains universally loved, which is either a testament to his charm or proof that this league has very low standards for attendance.

Sam is fourth, which is excellent, and Sam would celebrate this if he weren't already celebrating something else — a pint, a song, probably both. His brother Wigs is tenth, which means the family WhatsApp is doing something interesting right now. Wigs, to his credit, is the sort of man who would counsel you through the pain of a bad fantasy week with such warmth and insight that you'd feel genuinely better about it — and then you'd remember he's also in the league and you'd feel slightly suspicious. The brothers share a love of cricket, which means they are both already mentally checked out waiting for a different sport entirely.

Jeremy is fifth, picking his team the way a sommelier picks a wine — by instinct, experience, and a quiet, unshakeable confidence that he simply knows. He built the spreadsheet this entire league runs on. He coded it from scratch. He could, if he wanted, build an algorithm that would make Chris's look like a broken abacus. He has chosen not to. He watches Lloydy's AI adventures with the expression of a professor watching a student microwave a textbook. Speaking of which — Chris, ninth, whose algorithm recently held the entire league's transfer window hostage while it filed five consecutive bids for players from the 2022 World Cup. Five. In a row. The players it tried to sign are not here. Some of them have retired. The algorithm was, in effect, holding a séance. It did eventually land one valid bid, and that player scored 8 points, which Chris is still dining out on. Jake, twelfth, is not dining out on anything. Jake is furious. Jake has been furious since the transfer window, furious about Jeremy, furious about Chris, furious in the abstract. He injured himself recently — details unclear, though knowing Jake it was probably on the way to the dentist, which he loves, genuinely loves, more than most people love anything. 'SNACOB'S LADDER!' he shouts, periodically, to no one. He is also, somewhere in a different field, playing the exact same music as Tom.

Dave is eleventh, and he's fine with that, because Dave has reframed eleventh as a cultural position rather than a sporting one. He's working on the pitch. He has thoughts. He has puns. He has a dream of a television segment called Trossy's Wafflecock in which he sits in a nice chair and says important-sounding things about nothing in particular until everyone feels vaguely enriched. The cymbals are waiting. Dan, seventh, is off-grid and entirely unbothered — the most quietly dangerous manager in the league, historically sharp, allied with Malik in thirteenth, who is managing from Iceland and has signed everyone who has ever worn a red shirt for Manchester United or pulled on a Portugal kit. Portugal, of course, have been eliminated. Malik has taken this with the serene equanimity of a man who saw it coming and signed them anyway, on principle.

So there we are. Thirteen managers. No football. Just standings, grudges, a spreadsheet, a séance-algorithm, a dentist, two brothers, and one man somewhere in a field who may or may not know what day it is.

The next fixtures will arrive when they arrive — and when they do, the points will flow, the standings will shift, and Jake will shout his team name at least once before the first whistle. The Morning After will be waiting.
