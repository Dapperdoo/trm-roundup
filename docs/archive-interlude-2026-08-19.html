THE MORNING AFTER

Interlude Special
No football. Just thirteen managers. God help us.

Right. There are no fixtures today. The World Cup has paused for breath, the players are icing their hamstrings, and we — all of us, machine and man alike — are left staring at a blank schedule. So we're turning the spotlight away from the pitch and onto the thirteen people who've been sweating over it. Pull up a chair. This is the room. Let's work it.

Let's start at the top, because Joe A is sitting on 461 points and an expression of mild, Guinness-assisted surprise. He didn't dominate the transfer window — he made a few decent signings and the rest of the league was kind enough to not immediately punish him for it. Now Tristan is breathing down his neck at 364, jaw set, hands already halfway through the air in pre-emptive disgust. These two have the energy of a man who's been told his table is ready and the other man who's been waiting longer and absolutely clocked it. Joe A's other great project, for those keeping score, is trying to get AI tools to actually do what they advertise. He has not yet succeeded. He is, however, very persistent, which is more than can be said for the AI.

Speaking of persistence in the face of futility: Nick is in second place, which is remarkable given that every shot he takes appears to have been struck with a nine iron from the rough, aimed at a green three fairways over. Somehow the ball keeps landing near the hole. On the football pitch, Nick is a different proposition entirely — a reckless, dangerous, badly-timed blur of flying studs and cheerful menace, leaving opponents in a crumpled heap while he jogs away looking faintly puzzled. His main rival Tom, currently eighth on 321, may or may not be aware of this, depending on which field he's in and how the signal is.

Sam — fourth, 361 points, Look at his Face, Just Look at his FACE — is a man of genuine culture: Shakespeare, cricket, theatre, beer, dancing. The full Renaissance package. He once signed a year-long lease on a house because the viewing had its own bar, moved in on day one, and discovered there was no bathroom. He stood in the empty bar, surrounded by barrels, and made his peace with the situation in the way only Sam can: with tremendous good humour and absolutely no regret whatsoever. His brother Wigs is one place below him in tenth, which means the O'Shea family dinner table currently has a mild but unspoken competitive tension running underneath the gravy. Wigs — counsellor, cricket lover, genuinely one of the warmest people in this league — is handling it with characteristic grace. Sam is handling it by not mentioning it and hoping nobody notices.

Dave is in eleventh on 300 points, composing a poem about it. The poem will be good. It will contain a pun. It will be performed to cymbal accompaniment on a special occasion that Dave alone will determine. He still dreams of his Culture Show segment — 'Trossy's Wafflecock' — in which he would sit in a tastefully lit studio and talk at length about cultural nothingness with the absolute sincerity of a man who has fully convinced himself it matters. Dave, for what it's worth: it matters. Probably.

Jeremy is fifth on 360 points, which is quietly excellent and arrived without any algorithm, any AI, any system, or any of the general digital theatre that has afflicted this league. He picked his team on instinct, taste and thirty years of accumulated football knowledge, and it is working. He built the spreadsheet that underpins this entire operation — the one Tristan still runs off — and has the calm, slightly withering air of a man who could have automated everything years ago and chose, deliberately, not to. He is aware of what happened with Lloydy's algorithm. He has said very little about it. The silence is eloquent.

Which brings us to Chris. Lloydy. Ninth place, 320 points, and the owner of the most chaotic transfer window in recent memory. His algorithm — lovingly assembled in what we can only imagine is a shed full of blinking lights and exposed wiring — submitted five consecutive bids for players from the 2022 World Cup before anyone could stop it. Five. The whole league's window ground to a halt while Lloydy's machine held a séance for the class of four years ago. It did eventually land one valid bid, and that player scored 8 points, so the algorithm is technically not a complete disaster. It is, however, a disaster. Jake has not stopped talking about it. Jake will never stop talking about it. Jake is, right now, wherever he is, talking about it.

Jake. Twelfth. 292 points. Snacob's Ladder — which he will tell you himself, loudly and without prompting: 'SNACOB'S LADDER!' He spent a king's ransom on Harry Kane and filled the remaining slots with players whose names even he occasionally has to look up. He is also, medically speaking, a catastrophe. The man injured himself on the way to the dentist. Not at the dentist. On the way there. He loves the dentist, by the way — genuinely, enthusiastically, in a way that raises questions — so the injury was doubly cruel. He is currently nursing a grudge against Jeremy over the recent transfer correction that every other manager accepted and moved on from in approximately four minutes. Jake has not moved on. Jake is keeping receipts. Jake will be keeping receipts at the dentist, on the dancefloor, and while slopping an entire tray of drinks over someone who only came out for a quiet one.

At the bottom, Malik — 280 points, managing from Iceland with the serene confidence of a man who has a system: sign anyone who has ever worn a Manchester United or Portugal shirt. Portugal, incidentally, are eliminated. Malik is fine. Dan, his ally in seventh on 329, is living off-grid, playing music, and doing better than most people who are actively trying. These two operate on a frequency the rest of the league can't quite tune into, and it seems to be working for at least one of them. Finally, Joe S — sixth, 338 points, Back of the Van United — a man of genuine footballing genius whose own body spent most of his playing career actively conspiring against him. The treatment table saw more of Joe S than the pitch did. He has survived it all with charm, style and absolutely no interest in whatever app you're trying to show him. He is the best footballer in this league and he will never, ever check his phone to find out how he's doing.

The next fixtures will be here before you know it — and when they arrive, the points will flow, the standings will shift, and thirteen managers will once again have something to shout about besides each other. Some of them will be ready. Some of them will be on a dancefloor. One of them will be at the dentist.

No fixtures are confirmed yet for the next round — but the World Cup Final is on the horizon, and when it lands, this bulletin will be back, numbers in hand, ready to report exactly who cleaned up and who has to explain themselves. Rest up, managers. The football is coming.
